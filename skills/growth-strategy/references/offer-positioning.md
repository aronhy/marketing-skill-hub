
# Offer & Positioning for Overseas Markets

## Goal

A one-sentence positioning statement, three testable offers, and answers to the buyer's main objections.

## Before you start: gather context

- Product, price, margin, what makes it different (in the user's own words)
- Target customer and their main problem
- Competitor prices and offers (use `references/competitor-ad-research.md` if unknown)
- Current conversion rate and what customers say, if available

## Workflow

1. **Positioning statement.** Fill in: For [specific customer] who [problem], [product] is the [category] that [key outcome], unlike [alternative] because [reason to believe].
2. **Outcome over features.** Rewrite every feature as the result the customer gets. "Stainless steel" becomes "won't rust after a year of daily use."
3. **Build three offers** to test, for example:
   - Straight price with free shipping
   - Bundle (buy 2 save X%, or a starter kit) to raise AOV
   - Risk reversal: extended guarantee, free returns, try-before-you-commit
4. **Objection map.** List the top five reasons a US buyer would hesitate (price, quality, shipping time, trust in an unknown brand, fit or size) and the proof that answers each.
5. **Proof inventory.** Reviews, ratings, user photos, press, certifications, numbers sold, guarantees. Mark what exists and what to collect.

## US buyer expectations

- Free shipping, often above a threshold; clear delivery estimate
- 30-day returns or better
- Visible reviews with photos
- Real contact information and a US-friendly support experience
- Honest, specific claims; vague superlatives reduce trust

## Rules of thumb

- If ads get a good CTR but conversion is low, the problem is usually the offer or the page, not the targeting.
- Raise perceived value before lowering price. Bundles and guarantees protect margin better than discounts.
- Never invent reviews, fake scarcity or fake "was" prices. They break US consumer protection rules and ad policies.

## Output format

1. Positioning statement
2. Feature → outcome table
3. Three offers with price, what's included, and the expected effect
4. Objection map: objection, proof, where it appears (ad, page, email)
5. Proof inventory with gaps to fill
