# Where Do Goods Ship From? (货从哪里发)

Ask this early. The answer changes delivery promises, unit economics, ad copy, landing page, emails and customer service scripts. Record it in `.agents/product-context.md` under **Fulfillment**.

## The question

> Where will orders ship from?
> A. Directly from China (self-fulfilled, dropshipping, or a China-based fulfillment agent)
> B. Stock already in the destination market (US warehouse / 3PL, Amazon FBA, or FBA inventory used for website orders via Amazon Multi-Channel Fulfillment)
> C. Not decided yet

If C, recommend: start with A to test products with low risk, then move proven winners to B.

## Path A: Ship from China (中国直发)

Typical delivery: about 7–15 days to the US. Low inventory risk, slower delivery.

| Area | What changes |
|---|---|
| Unit economics | Add longer-transit costs: higher "where is my order" support load, more refunds and chargebacks. Confirm current US duties for the product's HTS code; small-parcel duty rules have changed. |
| Offer | Lead with risk reversal (guarantee, tracking) to offset the wait. Bundles help absorb shipping cost. |
| Ads | Don't imply fast shipping. Avoid angles that depend on speed ("arrives tomorrow"). Urgency around holidays must respect real cut-off dates. |
| Landing page | Show the honest delivery window near the price and at checkout. |
| Email | Use the shipping-update flow (`email-marketing` → `references/flow-copy-examples.md`). |
| Customer service | Proactively state delivery time in chat; prepare a delay script (`conversational-sales`). |
| Holidays | Set Christmas order cut-off early (roughly two to three weeks before) and switch messaging after it. |
| Next step | Once a product sells consistently, test a US warehouse or FBA; faster delivery usually raises conversion rate. |

## Path B: Ship from local stock (海外仓 / 3PL / FBA)

Typical delivery: about 2–5 days. Faster and converts better; needs inventory and cash up front.

| Option | Good for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| US warehouse / 3PL | Website (Shopify) brands with steady sales | Storage fees, minimum volumes, inventory planning |
| Amazon FBA | Selling on Amazon with Prime eligibility | Inventory limits, storage and long-term fees, Amazon's rules |
| FBM (seller-fulfilled on Amazon) | Low-volume or bulky items, testing on Amazon | Delivery speed and Prime eligibility suffer if shipping from China |
| Amazon Multi-Channel Fulfillment | Using FBA stock for website orders too | Fees and packaging rules; check current Amazon policy |

| Area | What changes |
|---|---|
| Unit economics | Add inbound freight, storage, pick-and-pack fees; model cash tied up in stock. |
| Offer | Fast delivery becomes a selling point ("ships from the US in 1–2 days"). |
| Ads | Speed and convenience angles work; holiday cut-offs are later. |
| Inventory | Reorder before stock-outs; a stock-out pauses ads and resets momentum. Plan Q4 stock by early autumn. |

## Quick comparison

| | China direct | Local stock (3PL / FBA) |
|---|---|---|
| Delivery to US | ~7–15 days | ~2–5 days |
| Upfront inventory | None or low | Required |
| Conversion rate | Lower | Higher |
| Support load | Higher (WISMO) | Lower |
| Best stage | Testing products | Scaling proven winners |

Platform fees and duty rules change often; confirm current numbers before finalizing plans.
