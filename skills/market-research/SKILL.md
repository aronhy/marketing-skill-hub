# Overseas Market Research

## Goal

A go / no-go verdict backed by demand evidence, a clear target customer, realistic unit economics and a 14-day test plan.

## Before you start: gather context

- Product, category, cost of goods, planned retail price
- Fulfillment: ship from China, US warehouse, Amazon FBA, or dropshipping
- Who the user believes the customer is
- Budget for testing

## Workflow

1. **Demand signals.** Collect evidence from several sources, not one:
   - Search demand: keyword volume and trend direction (Google Trends, keyword tools)
   - Marketplace demand: Amazon best-seller rank, number and recency of reviews
   - Ad activity: how many brands run active ads in Meta Ad Library and TikTok Creative Center
   - Conversation: Reddit, TikTok and YouTube discussions, complaints and questions
2. **Target customer.** Define one primary customer: age range, life situation, the problem they feel, what triggers a purchase, top three objections.
3. **Competitor landscape.** List 5–10 competitors with price, positioning, main offer, channels and review themes. Hand off ad-level research to `competitor-ad-research`.
4. **Price benchmarks.** Where do competitors cluster? Is there room for a premium or a value position?
5. **Unit economics.** Build the table: price, COGS, shipping, duties and tariffs, payment fees (~3%), returns allowance, gross margin, break-even ROAS, target CPA.
6. **Regulatory check.** Flag categories that need extra compliance (FDA for food, supplements, cosmetics and medical devices; FCC for electronics; CPSC for children's products).
7. **Verdict and test plan.**

## Rules of thumb

- Evidence over opinion. Every claim about demand needs a source.
- If nobody advertises in the category, find out why before calling it a blue ocean. Often there is no money in it.
- Paid-social DTC usually needs gross margin of roughly 60% or more to survive ad costs. Below that, look at higher AOV bundles or organic channels.
- US tariffs and de minimis rules have changed often; tell the user to confirm current rates for their product code before finalizing the numbers.

## Common mistakes for sellers new to the US

- Pricing too low. In the US, a very cheap price often signals low quality.
- Ignoring delivery expectations. US shoppers compare against fast shipping; state realistic delivery times.
- Translating Chinese positioning literally instead of researching how Americans describe the problem.
- Forgetting returns, sales tax and chargebacks in the margin.

## Output format

1. Demand evidence table: source, signal, what it suggests
2. Target customer profile
3. Competitor table: brand, price, positioning, offer, channels, review themes
4. Unit economics table
5. Compliance flags
6. Verdict: Go / Go with changes / No-go, with reasons
7. 14-day validation test plan
